\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) augmented with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) suffer a significant loss in performance when addressing multi-hop relational queries over complex biological and scientific knowledge graphs. Classical GraphRAG models rely on Markovian random walks such as Personalized PageRank, which lose diffusion probability at a rate proportional to $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{t})$ and become trapped in high-degree hub nodes. This paper proposes \textbf{Q-GraphRAG}, a hybrid quantum-classical retrieval architecture that deploys Continuous-Time Quantum Walks (CTQW) over graph Heisenberg spin Hamiltonians. Exploiting quantum superposition and constructive interference in a $2^N$-dimensional Hilbert space enables ballistic wavepacket propagation ($\mathcal{O}(t)$) across candidate subgraphs. We evaluate Q-GraphRAG across a 50-item benchmark spanning Clinical Oncology, Precision Medicine, Antimicrobial Resistance, and Quantum Physics. Across a controlled 4-way ablation (Pure Vector, Classical GraphRAG, Classical RWR on identical pruned subgraphs, and Q-GraphRAG), Q-GraphRAG demonstrates statistical equivalence to classical baselines on Coverage-F1, the primary retrieval metric ($0.218 \pm 0.065$ vs.\ $0.222\text{--}0.226$, $p > 0.05$). Furthermore, it shows numerically higher, though not statistically significant, Answer Relevance ($0.977 \pm 0.055$, $p = 0.075\text{--}0.13$) and Faithfulness ($0.268 \pm 0.160$, $p = 0.38\text{--}0.76$) across comparisons (paired $t$-test, $N = 50$, Cohen's $|d| \le 0.26$). Hardware feasibility is demonstrated on an IBM Quantum Heavy-Hex superconducting QPU, achieving an experimental state fidelity of $\mathcal{F} = 0.9926$ on a 4-qubit Trotterized circuit.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Quantum GraphRAG, Continuous-Time Quantum Walk, Knowledge Graphs, Multi-Hop Reasoning, Precision Medicine, NISQ Hardware.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The application of advanced computational models---which include both Large Language Models (LLMs) and deep neural networks---can substantially enhance the process of automated knowledge extraction and assist with biomedical decision-making \cite{lewis2020retrieval}. Unfortunately, there is a high rate of diagnostic errors and factual hallucination associated with the exclusive reliance on parametric memory when using LLMs to analyze complex biological mechanisms of diseases, such as oncological signaling pathways. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) provides a principled mechanism to mitigate hallucinations by dynamically retrieving contextually relevant passages from external repositories prior to answer synthesis \cite{lewis2020retrieval,edge2024graphrag}.

Although RAG has demonstrated widespread success in single-hop semantic look-up tasks, the traditional paradigm of dense vector retrieval (i.e., cosine similarity over dense neural embeddings) exhibits fundamental drawbacks in multi-hop relational reasoning. For example, in complex biomedical domains characterized by interconnected oncological signaling cascades (e.g., chromosomal translocations $\rightarrow$ chimeric kinase formation $\rightarrow$ downstream pathway activation $\rightarrow$ targeted drug inhibition), the appropriate clinical response cannot be localized within a single document chunk. Rather, critical causal dependencies are distributed across disparate topological nodes in a Knowledge Graph (KG).

To overcome these structural limitations, Graph-Augmented RAG (GraphRAG) systems integrate structured Knowledge Graphs with dense vector stores. However, the majority of current state-of-the-art classical methods for graph retrieval---such as Personalized PageRank (PPR) and Breadth-First Search (BFS) expansions---rely on classical diffusion processes. Classical diffusion processes are inherently Markovian and diffusive: the probability amplitude associated with reaching an $H$-hop target node decays exponentially with distance ($\mathcal{O}(1/H)$), while probability mass becomes excessively trapped in high-degree hub entities (e.g., generic biomedical terms such as ``protein'' or ``cell'').

This paper introduces \textbf{Q-GraphRAG}, a novel hybrid quantum-classical retrieval architecture that utilizes unitary \textbf{Continuous-Time Quantum Walks (CTQW)} \cite{childs2002continuous,childs2003exponential} in place of classical random-walk diffusion. By mapping candidate multi-hop subgraphs into parameterized Heisenberg spin Hamiltonians, Q-GraphRAG exploits quantum state superposition and constructive interference of wavepackets to facilitate ballistic wavepacket propagation across the diameter of the graph ($\mathcal{O}(t)$). Consequently, it enables the discovery of deep entity relationships beyond the reach of classical random walk methods without suffering from hub-node entrapment.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Quantum Retrieval Architecture:} We formulate a hybrid retrieval framework that leverages Continuous-Time Quantum Walks over graph-derived Heisenberg Hamiltonians to accelerate topologically directed exploration in Knowledge Graphs.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Multi-Domain Benchmark ($N = 50$):} We evaluate retrieval performance across 50 multi-hop questions spanning Clinical Oncology (PubMedQA \cite{jin2019pubmedqa}), Precision Medicine (PrimeKG \cite{chandak2023building}), Antimicrobial Resistance (ChEMBL \cite{gaulton2012chembl}), and Quantum Physics. In a 4-way ablation with an identical-subgraph classical random-walk baseline, Q-GraphRAG achieves statistical equivalence on Coverage-F1 (our primary metric, $p > 0.05$) and produces numerically higher, though not statistically significant, Answer Relevance ($0.977 \pm 0.055$) and Faithfulness ($0.268 \pm 0.160$) compared to classical baselines ($p > 0.05$, Cohen's $|d| \le 0.26$).
    \item \textbf{Physical NISQ Proof-of-Concept:} We demonstrate hardware execution feasibility by compiling Trotterized graph Hamiltonian evolution circuits into OpenQASM~2.0 on an IBM Quantum Heavy-Hex superconducting QPU, achieving an experimental state fidelity of $\mathcal{F} = 0.9926$ on a 4-qubit proof-of-concept run.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Graph-Augmented RAG and Classical Restrictions}
Recent RAG research has addressed the restrictions of dense embedding representations using structured knowledge graphs \cite{karpukhin2020dense,pan2024unifying}. Edge et al. \cite{edge2024graphrag} introduced Microsoft's GraphRAG to retain graph topology and apply hierarchical community detection for global summarization; however, macro-level community summaries often aggregate away specific multi-hop causal paths (such as direct kinase--drug interactions). Yasunaga et al. \cite{yasunaga2021qagnn} combined graph neural networks with language models for knowledge-grounded reasoning, but message-passing architectures suffer from exponential neighbor expansion ($\mathcal{O}(d^H)$), making deep multi-hop traversals computationally heavy. 

Classical random-walk diffusion algorithms, such as Personalized PageRank (PPR) \cite{haveliwala2002topic}, are widely employed for graph-based candidate selection. However, classical random walks on complex scale-free networks exhibit severe ``hub-trapping'' \cite{noh2004random,newman2010networks}, where probability mass becomes heavily concentrated in high-degree connector nodes (e.g., generic biomedical terms like ``protein'' or ``cell''), exponentially suppressing the detection of specific low-frequency target entities ($\mathcal{O}(1/H)$).

\subsection{Continuous-Time Quantum Walks and Wavepacket Dynamics}
Continuous-Time Quantum Walks (CTQWs), originally formulated by Farhi and Gutmann \cite{farhi1998quantum} and Childs et al. \cite{childs2002continuous,childs2003exponential}, provide a unitary quantum alternative to Markovian diffusion. In a CTQW, wavepackets propagate ballistically ($\mathcal{O}(t)$) rather than diffusively ($\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{t})$), as reviewed extensively by Kempe \cite{kempe2003quantum} and M{\"u}lken and Blumen \cite{mulken2011continuous}. Meyer and Wong \cite{meyer2015connectivity} and Chakraborty et al. \cite{chakraborty2016spatial} proved that quantum walks can achieve optimal spatial search across irregular network topologies without requiring global graph symmetry. Faccin et al. \cite{faccin2013degree} analyzed quantum state dynamics on complex networks, demonstrating that quantum interference alters steady-state degree occupation compared to classical random walks. To the best of our knowledge, this work represents the first integration of CTQW into an operational LLM/RAG pipeline over structured domain knowledge bases.

\subsection{Quantum Graph Kernels and Hybrid QML}
Quantum graph representations and kernel methods have seen rapid development. Huang et al. \cite{huang2022quantum} proved information-theoretic quantum advantage for learning from physical quantum states. Schuld and Killoran \cite{schuld2019quantum} established the foundation for quantum feature spaces and statevector overlap kernels. Verdon et al. \cite{verdon2019quantum} introduced Quantum Graph Neural Networks for learning over graph structures. To avoid the costly optimization loops and barren plateaus common in parameterized quantum circuits \cite{cerezo2021cost}, Q-GraphRAG employs parameter-free physical Hamiltonian time evolution ($e^{-iHt}$). Statevector simulation packages like PennyLane \cite{bergholm2018pennylane} and TensorFlow Quantum \cite{broughton2020tensorflow} enable GPU-accelerated quantum simulation, with $N \le 14$ qubits representing a practical boundary for low-latency classical emulation before $\mathcal{O}(2^N)$ memory limits become prohibitive.

\subsection{Biomedical Benchmarks and Research Gap}
Biomedical question answering presents demanding multi-hop relational challenges. Jin et al. \cite{jin2019pubmedqa} introduced PubMedQA for biomedical reasoning, where answers frequently require synthesizing disparate evidence across publications. Chandak et al. \cite{chandak2023building} developed PrimeKG, a comprehensive precision medicine knowledge graph linking diseases, drugs, phenotypes, and molecular pathways. Gaulton et al. \cite{gaulton2012chembl} provide the ChEMBL repository of bioactivity and drug-target interactions. Evaluating such systems requires multidimensional assessment, for which Es et al. \cite{es2023ragas} established the RAGAS evaluation framework (Faithfulness, Answer Relevance).

\section{Dataset Description and Topological Data Preparation}\label{sec:dataset_and_preprocessing}

\subsection{Multi-Domain 50-Item Research Benchmark}
For the purpose of evaluating the performance of Q-GraphRAG's ability to identify multi-hop reasoning capabilities across complex and non-local scientific relations among a wide variety of datasets, we developed a complete 50-item benchmark dataset ($N=50$) across four specialized areas of study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical Oncology \& Targeted Therapeutics (15 items):} Focuses on targeted molecular therapies for cancer, gatekeeper resistance mutations, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), and synthetic lethality. Examples include: Trastuzumab Emtansine (T-DM1) cytotoxic drug delivery in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer; Osimertinib covalent C797 binding targeting EGFR L858R/T790M gatekeeper mutations; Sotorasib irreversible switch-II pocket inhibition of KRAS G12C; Olaparib synthetic lethality in BRCA1/2-deficient homologous recombination repair cancers; and Pembrolizumab PD-1 checkpoint blockade restoring CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell function.
    \item \textbf{Precision Medicine \& Causal Signaling Pathways (15 items):} Generated from the PrimeKG precision medicine knowledge base \cite{chandak2023building}, this portion includes multi-hop disease--gene--drug causal pathways and regulatory feedback loops. Examples include: BCR-ABL1 fusion tyrosine kinase $\rightarrow$ STAT5 phosphorylation $\rightarrow$ anti-apoptotic BCL-XL transcription $\rightarrow$ Imatinib ATP-pocket inhibition; BRAF V600E oncogenic signaling via MEK1/2 and ERK phosphorylation $\rightarrow$ dual inhibition (Dabrafenib / Trametinib); and SGLT2 receptor inhibition $\rightarrow$ AMPK activation $\rightarrow$ cardiovascular protection (Dapagliflozin).
    \item \textbf{Antimicrobial Resistance \& Microbial Genomics (10 items):} Generated from antimicrobial resistance enzymes generated by horizontal gene transfer, ChEMBL bioactivity databases \cite{gaulton2012chembl}, and microbial genomics, focusing on plasmids and resistance mechanisms. Examples include: Horizontal gene transfer of $bla_{KPC}$ and NDM-1 metallo-$\beta$-lactamase carbapenemases; colistin resistance mediated by $mcr\text{-}1$ phosphoethanolamine transferase; and upregulation of AdeABC tripartite multidrug efflux pump in \textit{Acinetobacter baumannii}.
    \item \textbf{Quantum Condensed Matter \& Quantum Information (10 items):} Covers topics including quantum information theory, relativistic electronic structures, and condensed matter physics. Examples include: Relativistic Dirac fermions and room-temperature Quantum Hall Effect in 2D Graphene honeycomb lattices; microscopic theory of Cooper pairing via phonon lattice vibrations in Bardeen--Cooper--Schrieffer (BCS) superconductivity; and Trotter--Suzuki discretized Heisenberg spin Hamiltonian evolution on superconducting qubits.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ground-Truth Answer Curation and Validation Protocol}
To provide rigorous evaluations of multi-hop retrieval and answer synthesis, we created a pair of expert validated answers for each benchmark question; one of which is the correct ground truth answer, and the other being an annotated reference sub-graph that includes gold entity nodes as well as relational edges needed to support all entities within the reasoning chain. Gold path annotations and factual answers were developed in collaboration with two independent domain annotators who referenced credible sources such as: The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology and PubMed literature for clinical oncology; UniProt and PrimeKG \cite{chandak2023building} for signaling cascade information; ChEMBL \cite{gaulton2012chembl} for bioactivity data; and peer-reviewed scientific literature related to condensed matter physics. High levels of inter-annotator agreement were established for gold entity sets and multi-hop paths using Cohen's Kappa coefficient ($\kappa=0.86$); however, ambiguous issues or edge cases were jointly adjudicated by a senior researcher before conducting an evaluation.

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Extraction and Seed Node Mapping}
All domain knowledge sources were converted into property multi-edge directed graphs denoted as $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, \mathcal{R})$, where $\mathcal{V}$ represents the collection of named entity nodes ($|\mathcal{V}|=N$), $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V}$ represents the collection of directed edges between these nodes, and $\mathcal{R}$ represents the collection of possible relation types. The Agentic Router employs zero-shot entity extraction via the upstream LLM to identify primary named entities in each incoming user query $Q$. We acknowledge that this extraction step is an unvalidated upstream component; however, because it serves as the identical upstream feeder across all four comparative pipelines, any extraction errors or omissions affect all conditions equally without introducing comparative bias. Identified entities are mapped to root seed nodes $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathcal{V}$, and local candidate $k$-hop subgraphs $\mathcal{G}_{\text{sub}} = (\mathcal{V}_{\text{sub}}, \mathcal{E}_{\text{sub}})$ are extracted around each seed ($k = 2..3$).

\subsection{Topological Qubit Pruning and Shortest-Path Bridging}
Memory usage grows exponentially as $\mathcal{O}(2^N)$ ($16 \times 2^N$ bytes) for simulating quantum states on classical machines. Therefore, candidate subgraphs are topologically reduced to an active qubit budget of $N \le 14\text{ nodes}$ via a 3-stage pipeline: (1) \textbf{Centrality Scoring} with Personalized PageRank (PPR); (2) \textbf{Budget Filtering} retaining $\mathcal{V}_{\text{pruned}} = \text{Top-}N(\mathcal{V}_{\text{sub}})$; and (3) \textbf{Shortest-Path Bridges} re-inserting shortest-path edges to ensure weakly connected components remain bridged. Finally, nodes are ordered canonically into a symmetric adjacency matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ to guarantee permutation invariance.

\section{Theoretical Foundations and Quantum Algorithmic Formulation}\label{sec:quantum_formulation}

\subsection{Graph Heisenberg Hamiltonian Formulation}
Q-GraphRAG maps the canonical $N \times N$ adjacency matrix $A$ directly into an isotropic Heisenberg XY/XYZ spin interaction Hamiltonian on a $2^N$-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} \cong (\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes N}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{H} = -\gamma \sum_{i < j} A_{ij} \left( \hat{X}_i \hat{X}_j + \hat{Y}_i \hat{Y}_j + \hat{Z}_i \hat{Z}_j \right)
    \label{eq:hamiltonian_formulation}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{X}_i, \hat{Y}_i, \hat{Z}_i$ are Pauli spin operators acting on wire $i$, $A_{ij} \in [0, 1]$ represents the normalized relational coupling weight, and $\gamma = 0.5$ denotes the quantum hopping rate. Real symmetry of $A$ guarantees Hermiticity ($\hat{H} = \hat{H}^\dagger$) and unitary energy conservation.

\subsection{Initial State Preparation and Continuous-Time Quantum Walk}
Dense node features $\vec{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ are mapped to single-qubit rotation angles $\theta_i = \pi / (1 + \exp(-\text{mean}(\vec{v}_i)))$, preparing the register as $|\psi_0\rangle = \bigotimes_{i=1}^N R_y(\theta_i) |0\rangle^{\otimes N}$ with equal superposition across seed nodes $|\psi(0)\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} |s\rangle$. The continuous unitary time evolution operator $|\psi(t)\rangle = \exp(-i \hat{H} t) |\psi(0)\rangle$ drives ballistic wavepacket propagation:
\begin{equation}
    \langle x^2 \rangle_{\text{quantum}} \sim v^2 t^2 \implies x_{\text{ballistic}} = 2\gamma t \sim \mathcal{O}(t)
\end{equation}
which is quadratically faster than classical diffusion ($\langle x^2 \rangle \sim 2Dt \implies x_{\text{diff}} \sim \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{t})$).

\subsection{Trotter-Suzuki Discretization and Hilbert Kernel Similarity}
Non-commuting Pauli interaction terms are discretized into $m$ shallow Trotter steps ($m = 2..3$) via first-order Trotter-Suzuki decomposition:
\begin{equation}
    U(t) = \exp(-i \hat{H} t) \approx \left[ \prod_{i < j, A_{ij} > 0} \exp\left( i \frac{\gamma t}{m} A_{ij} (\hat{X}_i \hat{X}_j + \hat{Y}_i \hat{Y}_j + \hat{Z}_i \hat{Z}_j) \right) \right]^m + \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{t^2}{m}\right)
\end{equation}
preventing barren plateaus on NISQ hardware. Candidate subgraphs are ranked using the statevector overlap kernel fidelity in Hilbert space:
\begin{equation}
    K(G_{\text{query}}, G_{\text{cand}}) = \left| \langle \psi(G_{\text{query}}) \mid \psi(G_{\text{cand}}) \rangle \right|^2
\end{equation}

\section{System Architecture and Cloud-Native Engineering}\label{sec:system_architecture}

\subsection{Dual-Path Routing and Distributed Task Queue}
Q-GraphRAG utilizes an intelligent dual-path routing engine: simple factoid queries bypass quantum simulation and query \texttt{Qdrant} directly, while complex multi-hop queries are routed to the quantum pipeline. The \texttt{FastAPI} gateway dispatches quantum walk simulation tasks (\texttt{compute\_subgraph\_similarity\_task}) to background \texttt{Celery} workers running C++ PennyLane \texttt{lightning.qubit}. Computed transition kernels $K(G_{\text{query}}, G_{\text{cand}})$ are cached in \texttt{Redis} using SHA-256 hashes of canonical adjacency matrices, delivering warm hit latencies of less than 1 ms ($< 1$ ms) for the quantum retrieval kernel. While the isolated CTQW kernel computation executes in $<25$ ms ($<1$ ms on cache hit), the overall end-to-end pipeline latency ($\sim 2.2$ s, Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_50_results}) is predominantly governed by autoregressive generation in the downstream LLM (\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}) rather than retrieval execution.

\subsection{Context Assembly, Guardrails and 3D Visualization}
Top-ranked subgraphs are converted into Cypher relationship strings and JSON blocks, injected into the LLM (\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} / \texttt{Cloudflare Workers AI} / \texttt{Ollama}) with strict grounding prompts. Telemetry milestones are streamed in real time via bidirectional WebSocket (\texttt{/api/v1/ws/query}) to a client dashboard built with \texttt{React 18}, \texttt{TypeScript}, and \texttt{Three.js} (3D Force-Directed Graph), backed by \texttt{MongoDB Atlas} and \texttt{Firebase Authentication}.

\section{Experimental Evaluation and Discussion}\label{sec:experimental_evaluation}

\subsection{50-Item Multi-Domain 4-Way Ablation Benchmark}
We executed a 4-way comparative ablation study across all 50 benchmark items comparing: (1) Pure Vector search (\texttt{Qdrant}), (2) Classical GraphRAG (PageRank on the full knowledge graph), (3) Classical Random Walk with Restart (RWR, $\alpha=0.85$) on the \emph{identical} $N\leq14$ pruned subgraph used by Q-GraphRAG---a controlled ablation that isolates the CTQW kernel contribution from the topological pruning pipeline---and (4) \textbf{Q-GraphRAG} (CTQW on the $N\leq14$ pruned subgraph). In multi-hop scientific reasoning (e.g., traversing kinase inhibition cascades or resistance pathways), omitting an essential intermediate entity or causal relation disrupts the entire reasoning chain, whereas retrieving slightly broader context can still be filtered by the generator. Consequently, we designate \textbf{Coverage-F1} with recall weighting ($\beta = 1.5$: $F_{\beta} = (1+\beta^2)\frac{P \cdot R}{\beta^2 P + R}$) as our primary metric to prioritize relational entity recall, while reporting standard token-level Answer~F1 ($\beta = 1.0$) for direct comparability with conventional RAG benchmarks. Results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_50_results}.

\textbf{Discussion.}
All pairwise statistical tests comparing Q-GraphRAG to the three baselines using a paired $t$-test indicate statistical equivalence across retrieval and generation dimensions ($p > 0.05$, $N = 50$). Crucially, on our designated primary metric, Coverage-F1 ($\beta = 1.5$), Q-GraphRAG is numerically the lowest of all four configurations ($0.218 \pm 0.065$ vs.\ $0.225$ Pure Vector, $0.226$ Classical Graph, and $0.222$ Classical RWR), though these differences are statistically indistinguishable ($p = 0.26$, $d = -0.16$ vs.\ Classical Graph). Comparing to Classical GraphRAG: Faithfulness $\Delta = +0.004$ ($p = 0.76$, $d = 0.04$); Precision@5 $\Delta = +0.023$ ($p = 0.17$, $d = 0.20$). Comparing to Classical RWR on the identical pruned graph: Faithfulness $\Delta = +0.009$ ($p = 0.38$, $d = 0.13$); Precision@5 $\Delta = -0.002$ ($p = 0.78$, $d = -0.04$). 

For RAGAS Answer Relevance, Q-GraphRAG exhibits a consistent positive trend against all baselines: vs.\ Pure Vector ($\Delta = +0.027$, $t = 1.82$, $p = 0.075$, $d = 0.26$), vs.\ Classical Graph ($\Delta = +0.016$, $t = 1.66$, $p = 0.10$, $d = 0.24$), and vs.\ Classical RWR ($\Delta = +0.015$, $t = 1.54$, $p = 0.13$, $d = 0.22$). Although these differences reflect small-to-moderate positive effect sizes ($d \approx 0.22\text{--}0.26$), they remain statistically non-significant ($p > 0.05$). We note that across the 18 pairwise comparisons evaluated (6 metrics $\times$ 3 baseline comparisons), all tests are unadjusted for multiple comparisons; after family-wise error rate adjustment (e.g., Bonferroni $\alpha_{\text{adj}} \approx 0.0028$), borderline $p$-values ($p = 0.075\text{--}0.13$) are non-significant and must be treated as suggestive rather than confirmatory.

The minimal performance separation between Classical RWR on the identical pruned graph and Q-GraphRAG demonstrates that downstream generative quality is predominantly governed by the topological pruner and subgraph quality rather than the walk kernel at cold start. Regarding runtime, end-to-end latency across all methods hovers closely around $\sim 2.15\text{--}2.27$ seconds. This uniformity confirms that response time is overwhelmingly dominated by the autoregressive generation of the LLM (\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}), whereas retrieval kernel execution requires $<25$ ms ($<1$ ms on Redis cache hit), representing under $2\%$ of overall wall-clock latency.

This empirical parity warrants theoretical examination. Continuous-Time Quantum Walks theoretically offer ballistic wavepacket dispersion ($\mathcal{O}(t)$) compared to classical Markovian diffusion ($\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{t})$)---an advantage that grows with graph diameter $D$ and path length $H$. However, under our classical simulation budget of $N \le 14$ qubits, candidate subgraphs are topologically compact ($D \le 3$), compressing the dynamical separation between quantum unitary propagation and classical random walks. Furthermore, the 3-stage topological pruner (PPR + shortest-path bridging) resolves a significant portion of hub-trapping prior to walk execution. This indicates that the null result at $N=50$ is primarily a consequence of subgraph scale and cold-start density rather than an intrinsic limitation of quantum walk kernels. Isolating the predicted quantum speedup in end-to-end QA will require scaling beyond NISQ emulation limits to larger, denser multi-relational graphs ($N \ge 30\text{--}50$) where classical random walks experience severe probability dilution and hub congestion.

\subsection{Physical NISQ Superconducting Hardware Validation (IBM Quantum)}
Trotterized graph circuits compiled to \texttt{OpenQASM 2.0} were executed on IBM Quantum Heavy-Hex architectures (12 gates, single-qubit and CNOT gates). Evaluating measured basis probability distributions against ideal statevectors yields an experimental state fidelity of $\mathbf{\mathcal{F} = 0.9926}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:hardware_composite}). While this initial 4-qubit hardware demonstration proves the feasibility and high fidelity of Trotterized CTQW circuits with current superconducting QPU technology, this is a first small-scale proof of concept. Therefore, in order for it to be possible to use the entire $N \le 14$ qubits that are available for executing physically, advanced methods for mitigating errors will need to be developed to compensate for decoherence in the multi-qubit entangled layers.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis}\label{sec:sensitivity}
We examined how sensitive the CTQW retrieval kernel was to its hyperparameters as we varied both the hopping rate ($\gamma \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0\}$) and the number of Trotter-Suzuki discretization steps ($m \in \{2, 3, 4\}$) over a 10-item validation set that represented each of the 4 benchmark domains. Using $\gamma = 0.5$ is found to be the optimal trade-off between spreading out values within a graph with small diameter $D \le 3$ and reflecting values at the boundaries. At $\gamma = 0.25$, the walk under-diffuses, yielding lower Coverage-F1 ($0.209 \pm 0.058$). Conversely, increasing to $\gamma = 1.00$ induces excessive boundary scattering and destructive phase cancellation across compact topologies, reducing Coverage-F1 ($0.211 \pm 0.062$). Regarding Trotter discretization, increasing from $m = 2$ to $m = 3$ reduces the statevector Trotterization approximation error $\|U(t) - U_{\text{Trotter}}(t)\|_2$ from $4.2\%$ to $1.1\%$ with marginal latency overhead ($+3.4\text{ ms}$), while $m = 4$ yields diminishing returns ($0.6\%$ error at $+7.1\text{ ms}$). Thus, $\gamma = 0.50$ and $m = 3$ provide the optimal operating point (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity_analysis}).

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}\label{sec:limitations}

The system's limitations stem from classical memory scaling and NISQ noise:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Classical Statevector Memory ($\mathcal{O}(2^N)$):} Exact simulation of $2^N$-dimensional Hilbert space scales as $16 \times 2^N$ bytes. Bounding subgraphs to $N \le 14$ nodes ($262\text{ KB}$ RAM, $<25\text{ ms}$) is necessary on container nodes. Subgraphs $>14$ nodes undergo iterative topological partitioning.
    \item \textbf{NISQ Noise and Coherence ($T_1, T_2$):} Two-qubit gate error rates necessitate shallow Trotter steps ($m \le 3$) to maintain circuit fidelity.
    \item \textbf{Clinical AI Ethics:} Q-GraphRAG enforces strong topological grounding to suppress hallucinations, but is designed for research decision support and requires physician validation prior to patient care.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Directions}\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \textbf{Q-GraphRAG}, a hybrid quantum-classical Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework leveraging Continuous-Time Quantum Walks (CTQW) over graph Heisenberg spin Hamiltonians. Q-GraphRAG achieves ballistic wavepacket propagation ($\mathcal{O}(t)$), theoretically mitigating classical hub-trapping. Across our 50-item, 4-domain multi-hop benchmark, Q-GraphRAG showed statistical equivalence to classical retrieval baselines on our declared primary metric, Coverage-F1 ($0.218 \pm 0.065$ vs.\ $0.222\text{--}0.226$, all $p > 0.05$). It demonstrated numerically higher, though not statistically significant, Answer Relevance ($0.977 \pm 0.055$, $p = 0.075\text{--}0.13$) and Faithfulness ($0.268 \pm 0.160$, $p = 0.38\text{--}0.76$) compared to classical methods (paired $t$-test, Cohen's $|d| \le 0.26$). In our controlled ablation using the identical $N \le 14$ pruned graph with classical RWR, we observed that topological pre-processing accounts for the primary share of retrieval quality at small subgraph scales. Future work will investigate larger, pre-populated multi-relational knowledge graphs ($N \ge 30$) to isolate the full dynamical advantage of quantum walk kernels. Physical quantum validation on IBM Heavy-Hex QPUs demonstrated an experimental state fidelity of $\mathbf{\mathcal{F} = 0.9926}$ on a 4-qubit proof of concept. Future research directions include parameterized VQE graph embeddings, persistent cross-session knowledge graph construction, and Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing deployments.
